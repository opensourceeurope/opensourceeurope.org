## General Principles

Collectives hosted by Open Source Europe (OSE) are unincorporated initiatives primarily funded through donations. So they are not eligible for VAT refunds, and they must always pay VAT on the goods and services they buy.

VAT (Value Added Tax) is a general consumption tax that applies to most goods and services within the European Union. Even if your Collective is not a registered entity, VAT is still due on your expenses.

:::note
Learn more: [VAT explained – European Commission](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation-1/value-added-tax-vat_en)
:::

***

## VAT Within the Same Country

Invoices issued within your country (e.g. Belgium) must include VAT unless the supplier is legally exempt.

OCE itself is not VAT-exempt. If it issues an invoice, for example for services, VAT is applied at the applicable Belgian rate.

:::note
Belgian VAT details: [Finance Belgium](https://finance.belgium.be/en)
:::

***

## Cross-Border VAT and Reverse Charge

The reverse charge mechanism lets VAT-registered businesses handle cross-border VAT internally: they declare both output and input VAT.

However, this mechanism only applies between two VAT-registered businesses. OCE-hosted Collectives are not legal entities, so they cannot apply the reverse charge mechanism.

:::danger
This means you must pay VAT on cross-border invoices from EU-based suppliers, like any individual or unregistered group.
:::

:::note
Learn more: [EU VAT rules – Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/vat-rules-rates/index_en.htm)
:::

***

## Summary

* Collectives must pay VAT on all eligible purchases, whether national or cross-border.
* Reverse charge rules do not apply.
* If an invoice leaves out VAT but you are not VAT-registered, ask the vendor to correct it. Otherwise, justify the exemption clearly.
