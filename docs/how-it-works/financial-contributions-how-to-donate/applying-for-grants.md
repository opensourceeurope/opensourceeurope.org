Use this guide when a funder may support your Collective through Open Source Europe (OSE). Your Collective leads the application and the project. OSE reviews its own role and commitments.

## The grant journey

1. Before you submit, check the funder's requirements and contact OSE. If the application names OSE as recipient or signatory, OSE must review and accept that role before submission.
2. If you get the grant, share the grant agreement and arrange the necessary agreements and payment instructions with OSE.
3. When the funds arrive, use the Collective budget in line with the funding conditions. A promised grant is not an available balance.
4. During the project, prepare activity reports and project monitoring. OSE provides the accounting information it holds and validates anything that binds OSE.

The following sections give the details of the agreement, communication, and reporting requirements. See also [hosting fees](/docs/faq/general-faqs/#how-much-does-it-cost) and [spending the grant budget](/docs/how-it-works/expenses/).

## Grants through OSE

OSE enables hosted Collectives to receive grants from institutional and philanthropic funders, both in Europe and internationally. The OSE team helps you prepare the arrangements to receive and manage the grant.

:::danger
If a grant application names OSE as recipient or signatory, contact OSE before submission, so the team can review and accept OSE's role and commitments.
:::

***

## Initial steps

1. Contact OSE early

   Email <home@opensourceeurope.org> to tell OSE about the grant opportunity. The OSE team helps you check whether the grant is compatible with fiscal hosting through OSE.
2. Check the funder's requirements

   Some funders do not allow grants to fiscally sponsored initiatives. Ask the funder whether it accepts a hosted structure.
3. If your funder is in the U.S., arrange its documentation

   Contact the OSE team to arrange the documentation that your funder requires. For more information, see [Equivalency Determination Certificate](/docs/what-we-offer/equivalency-determination-certificate/).

***

## Grant agreements & receiving funds

1. Share the grant agreement

   The funder provides a grant agreement. The confirmed recipient entity must sign this agreement. Share the agreement with OSE at <home@opensourceeurope.org>, or ask the funder to contact OSE directly.
2. Sign the Mandate Agreement

   Your initiative also needs to sign a Mandate Agreement with OSE. This agreement makes the arrangement formal. One signatory from your initiative is enough.
3. Get the bank transfer instructions

   The OSE team provides the right bank transfer instructions for the agreed grant arrangement.
4. Check the available balance

   Once OSE receives the grant, the funds appear on your Collective’s Open Collective page as available balance.

***

## Funder communication

The following points cover how you communicate with funders:

* You can publicly recognize funders on your Collective page. A funder's name appears in your list of contributors unless the funder chooses to remain anonymous.
* Keep your funders informed with regular updates on the Open Collective platform.
* If funders send requests or messages to OSE, OSE forwards them to your initiative for follow-up.

***

## Grant reporting

The Collective prepares activity reports and project monitoring. OSE provides the accounting information it holds and validates statements or commitments that bind OSE. Follow the reporting requirements and deadlines of the grant agreement.

***

## Get help

The Collective leads its own fundraising. OSE may occasionally share opportunities or make introductions when it has capacity. OSE does not promise a structured fundraising service or funding success. For questions about how to receive and administer a grant, contact <home@opensourceeurope.org>.
