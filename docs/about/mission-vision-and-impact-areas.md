It all comes back to resilience.

## Mission

Open Source Europe (OSE) works to make the social and solidarity economy and open source technologies sustainable. The OSE mission is to enable initiatives to raise and spend money transparently under a common umbrella nonprofit.

*Open Collective is an unusual company. In its ecosystem, a software company coexists with independent associations and foundations known as 'hosts.' OSE is growing as one of the first major fiscal hosts on the European continent. OSE hosts initiatives that share its values and work toward a better future. The OSE values, and the OSE vision for a healthy and thriving world, start with resilience. From that central concern come six related areas where OSE works to make a difference.*

### Resilient communities

Humanity is divided on countless fronts, but most people agree on the need for resilience. When systems fail, people need fallback buffers. Resilient communities don't break when one system failure causes the next. They have a patchwork of fallbacks to absorb the shocks. Resilience is when people next to you on the street know how to give cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) or can swim to save someone from drowning. It is when the families in your neighborhood own their own homes. It is also when your community has enough nurses to deliver your children and enough teachers to educate them. It is when every other house in your neighborhood has solar panels that feed spare capacity back to the grid. When authorities arrive at a natural disaster, they find that the local community has already started helping itself. It is a society of ordinary people who are great leaders when they need to be. Resilience keeps people, jobs, and services in your hometown. When people can trust their community to catch them if they fall, they can safely turn their attention to working for the greater good. That is why OSE puts resilient communities at the center of its mission.

### Open innovation

When people use science and technology wisely, they become more resourceful. They expand their horizons and capabilities. If they share their findings, they grow stronger together. OSE encourages people to work together. Through the network effect, OSE helps knowledge and technology spread faster. Without this sharing, society risks becoming divided by increasing technological gaps. Civil society is key to making innovation available to all. Distributed networks of passionate people around the world develop open source software. Citizen scientists collect and share data. Civil engineers document how to build more energy-efficient homes. Technologists share blueprints for 3D printers and robots. Maker spaces around the world help people innovate and repair. Open innovation makes all of this possible.

### Commons stewardship

When a social innovation, cultural institution, or green space starts to feel like it has always been there, it has become a common good. Ideally, the commons that people create become so readily available that they appear unremarkable. At exactly this point, the common good becomes vulnerable. Humankind longs for what is new and exciting. People want to solve the urgent problems of the day, and that is where they focus their attention. But people also need to maintain the solutions to problems they have already solved. Once saved from deforestation, a forest needs protection from invasive species. When one generation builds a community center, the next must repair and maintain it. Someone must update the technology that encrypts websites and emails and keeps people safe from surveillance. Nobody can take the commons for granted. Otherwise, all the work on communities, solidarity, culture, innovation, and ecologies risks being undone. For this progress to last, commons stewardship needs support.
